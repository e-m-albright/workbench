### One-sentence positioning

> AI makes building faster. I make the result real by connecting the workflow, data, technology, economics, risk, and ownership.

### Facts to keep straight

- The goal is not an innovation theater pilot.
- The first system should prove value and teach the company how to own the next one.
- Transfer comes before scale so the builder does not become the permanent bottleneck.

## Opening

### Suggested opening

> I have spent my career finding important operating problems, building the first complete system, and leaving the organization able to run it. I would like to compare that pattern with the problems you see across the portfolio.

<div class="prompt">
<span class="label">Ask early</span>
<p>Where do you see capable teams repeatedly failing to turn an AI idea into a durable operating result?</p>
</div>

### Listen for

- A workflow with a measurable cost, delay, quality problem, or missed revenue opportunity.
- A named operator who owns the result.
- Friction between business, technology, compliance, and frontline incentives.
- A current initiative that is stuck between demonstration and production.

## Core story

### The pattern

1. **Find** an important workflow with a real owner and measurable baseline.
2. **Rank** opportunities by value, feasibility, risk, and transferability.
3. **Build** the smallest complete production system, not a disconnected demo.
4. **Pilot** in one bounded workflow with explicit success and stop criteria.
5. **Harden** reliability, security, monitoring, and operational recovery.
6. **Transfer** code, knowledge, controls, and decision rights to the company.
7. **Scale** only after value and ownership are proven.

### Proof story

<div class="evidence">
<span class="label">Situation</span>
<p>A fragmented workflow depended on manual coordination, inconsistent data, and slow handoffs.</p>
<span class="label">Action</span>
<p>I mapped the whole operating system, built the first end-to-end product, and introduced explicit quality and ownership gates.</p>
<span class="label">Result</span>
<p>The team moved from debating possibilities to operating a measurable system it could extend itself.</p>
</div>

## Questions that reveal the system

<div class="prompt"><span class="label">Value</span><p>Which operating problem would materially change the company if it were solved?</p></div>
<div class="prompt"><span class="label">Workflow</span><p>Where does the work actually slow down, break, or depend on heroics?</p></div>
<div class="prompt"><span class="label">Ownership</span><p>Who owns the outcome today, and who would own the system after a pilot?</p></div>
<div class="prompt"><span class="label">Evidence</span><p>What would have to move for the operator and investment team to call this successful?</p></div>
<div class="prompt"><span class="label">Risk</span><p>What failure would make the organization unwilling to expand the work?</p></div>

## Objections and controls

| Concern | Plain response | Evidence to request |
|---|---|---|
| “AI is moving too quickly.” | Keep the architecture replaceable and the workflow stable. | A model swap does not require rebuilding the product. |
| “Pilots never scale.” | Define production, ownership, and stop gates before the pilot begins. | Named owner, baseline, target, and operating handoff. |
| “The data is not ready.” | Test the minimum data path before committing to the use case. | Coverage, quality, permissions, and update cadence are known. |
| “Operators will not trust it.” | Put users in the design loop and make uncertainty visible. | Adoption and override behavior improve during the pilot. |
| “This could create compliance risk.” | Bound the workflow and assign human accountability explicitly. | Controls pass review before exposure expands. |

<div class="warning">
<span class="label">Do not say</span>
<p>Do not imply that every important workflow needs an agent, that model accuracy alone proves value, or that a fast prototype is evidence of production readiness.</p>
</div>

## Close

### Desired next step

> Let us pick one company and one workflow where the value is important, the owner is real, and a bounded test would teach us something decisive in a few weeks.

### Before ending

- Confirm the problem, owner, and reason it matters now.
- Name the evidence needed for a second conversation.
- Agree on who will provide context and when.
- Leave room for the other person to reshape the opportunity.
